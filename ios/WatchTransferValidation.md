# Paired-device phone transfer check

Build and install the same revision on the paired iPhone and Apple Watch. Record the revision, device models, OS versions, music filename and size, network path, and available storage. Use a track already downloaded on the phone but absent from the watch. Select its playlist for offline preparation on the watch; selecting playlists in phone settings alone only selects metadata.

In Xcode, set a source breakpoint on `onEvent?(job)` in `WatchFileDownloader.update`. Add the debugger action `po (Date(), job.transfer.id, job.transfer.generation, job.state.rawValue, job.reason?.rawValue, job.fraction)` and enable automatic continuation. Capture the initial request at the `transport.request` line and HTTP start at `startHTTP` using matching job IDs and timestamps. Alternatively, inspect the same job in the watch's application-support `files/phone-transfers.json`. Never record request tokens or cookies. The journal records pending preparation jobs and removes completed/cancelled jobs; it is not a history. Check `WCSession.outstandingFileTransfers` on the phone for the matching metadata and `progress.fractionCompleted`. The watch's accepted/transferring state is refreshed on activation/reconnection; a fraction of one on the phone does not by itself prove that the watch committed the file.

1. With both apps open, prepare one uncached track. Verify one phone transfer, a delivered event, the file in watch `files/music`, and a Ready playlist. Play it in airplane mode to confirm local playback.
2. Repeat with another uncached track. After the phone queues the transfer, lock/background the phone and lower the watch wrist. Allow more than 30 seconds. Reopen the watch and record the phone queue, delivery time, and playlist state. If HTTP completed first, verify the phone delivery does not replace the file or count it twice.
3. To isolate late phone delivery from HTTP, make the music endpoint temporarily unreachable from the watch after the phone accepts the request, retaining phone/watch connectivity. Let the foreground wait end. Verify that the journal retains the job and a subsequent matching phone delivery changes the playlist to Ready. Restore endpoint access afterwards.
4. With a job accepted and unfinished, background/terminate and reopen the watch app. Verify the same job ID survives, reconciliation reports the system transfer rather than queueing a duplicate, and its eventual delivery is usable.
5. While the phone is unreachable, cancel/remove the offline selection. Reconnect and let any old queued transfer arrive. Verify the file stays absent and reconciliation cancels the obsolete phone job. Repeat with sign-out or a changed server/token to verify that the old generation is rejected.
6. Play an uncached current track during preparation. Confirm playback uses the server stream immediately, retains next-track lookahead, and never waits for a phone transfer. Repeat with the phone backgrounded/locked and the watch wrist-down.

Record request acceptance, foreground deadline, HTTP start/result, actual phone delivery, cache commit, and audible playback separately. An accepted file that remains in Apple's queue is a scheduling delay until evidence shows a failure; acceptance and progress are not completion. Capture a transport error separately from a phone cache miss, queue-full rejection, stale generation, or storage error.

Simulator tests exercise the production transfer, storage and offline-library boundaries with controlled transports. They do not establish physical WatchConnectivity scheduling or wrist-down reliability. Apple documents asynchronous system-managed transfer scheduling and recommends paired-device testing: [transferFile](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/transferfile(_:metadata:)), [outstandingFileTransfers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/outstandingfiletransfers).
